# Four Nations Tactics: A World Worth Wandering

## Living-world and long-term story expansion

**Authored for Jared · 17 September 2026 · Companion draft 1.2 · Dialogue review 18 September 2026**

**Read alongside:** *Avatar RPG — The Common Current — Story Bible*, narrative revision 1.2 dated 18 September 2026 (the existing v1.0 filename is retained). Its Part 01 is the shared canon and era contract; Part 07 owns the dialogue and humor standard. This companion inherits both instead of creating a separate continuity or voice guide.

**Status:** new narrative development for review and later integration. The existing Bible remains the baseline. This companion deepens its people and places, proposes additional stories, and separates distant possibilities from the next playable slice. It does not report implemented game content. The available reading edition and this companion have been corrected together. The modular package was not found in the reviewed main-branch tree; these changes have not been applied to unlocated modules or committed to the repository. New IDs below are available in the reviewed reading edition and must be checked against the modular source before integration.

**Binding setting:** after Korra and before Seven Havens, before its world-shattering cataclysm, with the four nations, the United Republic, Republic City, spirits, and established Avatar animals still part of a living world. Exact dating is reserved. Republic City is a real named connection for trade, visitors, and the heroes’ pasts; Causeway remains an original regional city. Urban environments use Korra-era technology and Avatar’s animated visual language; rural places retain specific local traditions. These are authoring requirements, not claims that all destinations or assets are built.

**Revision 1.1:** withdraws the unsupported living lion-turtle encounter and retires SQ-35; replaces the earlier novel-fauna baseline; aligns Parcel and the source Bible; strengthens the wider-world and era rules. Original local residents, humor, branch consequences, and the four active new side stories remain.

**Revision 1.2:** applies the owner's request for stronger humor. Reworks the Deputy, lunch-boat, and meadow scenes around behavior and staging; trims narrator punchlines from character briefs; preserves quest IDs, outcomes, prerequisites, and the quiet Dalu visit. These remain authored scene proposals, not reports of implemented animation.

**The experience we are aiming for:** You follow a ridiculous animal down a path, meet somebody whose company you enjoy, discover that their life is more complicated than it first seemed, and eventually return because you care what happened. Sometimes you help. Sometimes you learn. Sometimes you simply spend a good afternoon there.

## 1. What the existing Bible gets right, and what to develop

The Common Current already has a coherent story: five missing residents lead the party from Ba Dan into a public-works network that helps people while denying them control over their lives. Veyra's useful achievements and deliberate overreach make a strong central conflict. The three endings preserve different workable answers. Keep that foundation.

Its quieter material is also promising. Parcel the delivery animal, the Bent Ladle lunch boat, Auntie Silt's favorite pebble, the ordinary fishing trip, and the moving market already point toward the world Jared wants. The work now is to give those ideas enough continuity to become remembered experiences.

Four opportunities stand out:

| Current strength | Development needed |
| --- | --- |
| Many well-designed ethical situations | More conversations whose immediate purpose is enjoying someone's company. People should have tastes, jokes, talents, and plans before they need correction. |
| Thirty side missions with return details | Several recurring relationships with intermediate visits, shared activities, changing preferences, and small invitations. A callback should sometimes begin a new afternoon. |
| Distinctive original local animals | Recognizable Avatar wildlife alongside those inventions, with habitat, behavior, relationships, and different degrees of rarity. |
| A complete campaign and political aftermath | An exploration structure that allows curiosity, recovery, and longer friendships to coexist with the investigation. |

The editorial risk is thematic repetition: another rule, another omission, another public meeting, another person learning to delegate. Those themes matter, but experiencing them repeatedly in the same shape can make the world feel like a series of lessons. Give room to competition, craft, hospitality, ridiculous pride, delight in an animal, difficult affection, and mysteries that remain partly mysterious.

**A useful writing test:** Would somebody want to talk to this person before learning they have a quest? Would the conversation still be enjoyable if it gave no clue, item, or approval increase?

## 2. Shape of the open world

### A connected region with several reasons to take each road

Keep the Tidemark Reach as the first substantial world. Its seven existing regions already support coast, town, marsh, orchard, mountain, and industrial spaces. Deepen their connections before adding whole nations.

After the opening introduces travel, the party can revisit accessible places and follow local interests. MQ-05 still opens the three investigative spokes; it need not be the first moment anyone can see an orchard overlook or hear a story about the heights. Separate access to a peaceful fringe from access to a mission's restricted interior when writing the maps.

Every substantial region needs several kinds of attraction: a landmark visible at a distance, a social place where people linger, a practical route through it, an optional side path, and at least one reason to come back after the urgent job ends. They do not all need map markers.

| Place | What draws you off the road | Who makes you stay | What changes on return |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ba Dan | Turtle ducks taking the inconvenient route between a spring pond and shade; little repaired stone ornaments | Dorin, Pella, Gao, and eventually Bo-shan | Guard routines, the siblings' game, seasonal pond edges, an occupied place at supper |
| Saltstep / Emberwatch | A lunch boat repositioned for the view; ostrich horses waiting for a ferry; vegetables growing through old military fittings | Pemi, Tavi, Miri, and the civilian keepers | New regulars, family visits, a different berth, the party's remembered meal |
| Causeway City | An absurd street performance and open-air games beneath working lamps | Haro, Dema, Dr. Iset, and people who disagree without ceasing to be neighbors | A new play, recurring opponents at the games, a seat offered without an errand attached |
| Copper Orchard | A freight-yard board game, Parcel's favorite rest stop, signs of animals underneath unused cuts | Parcel's handlers, Hesh, Renn, and visiting performers | New delivery routines, a practiced tune, an apprentice's independent work |
| Reedglass Marsh | Reeds full of light, frog calls, half-visible animal tracks, a reflection that deserves a second look | Fenn, Dalu, Vessa, Latch, Auntie Silt | Children's observations, the little stage, new water levels, a neighbor's current mood |
| Bellweather Heights | The shadow of a visiting sky bison, wind ribbons, a pasture and a sheltered tea ledge | Sena, Oru, Viri, students and caretakers | Travelers arriving and leaving, a shared game, a creature resting in a different place |
| Stillwater Works | Lunch whistles and small personal gardens beside an immense machine | Operators, cooks, cleaners, and local repair crews | The same people inhabiting a changed workplace under the selected ending |

### Exploration should feel invited

A conspicuously flattened reed bed can lead to an animal. Laughter can lead to a rehearsal. Parcel's silhouette can lead to a familiar handler. A person can mention a view because they like it, without secretly delivering a quest instruction.

When someone needs serious help, make that need legible. When someone merely offers company, let them mean it. Repeated kindness should not teach the player that every pleasant invitation conceals danger.

For a new hub's first visit, aim to include at least one interaction that is purely sociable, one optional discovery, and one substantial story invitation. Avoid introducing every named resident with a problem at once. This is an authoring guide, not a quota or a promise about minutes of play.

**Urgency remains explicit.** The existing final commitment before MQ-13 still controls the crisis. Wildlife observation, conversation, and reading do not secretly advance the flood. After commitment, defer leisurely scenes until travel is safe again. A pleasant fishing invitation does not interrupt an evacuation.

### The world continues without making the player its caretaker

NPCs can make progress on their own. A postponed concert can acquire a different performer. A delivery route can improve without the party receiving credit. A friendly person can leave a note and visit family. Declining ordinary company is not a moral failure, and returning after a break should not produce guilt or a list of neglected chores.

Remember concrete shared experiences: the joke the party chose, a game played, the seat they liked, a promise actually made. Do not reduce friendship to a universal numerical affection score.

## 3. Animals as neighbors, wildlife, and wonders

### Established species and original local placements

Lead with recognizable Avatar wildlife. The species below have established counterparts in the animated world; proposed local populations, named individuals, and encounters are original project placements. Detailed anatomy and exceptional abilities need the relevant animated reference before production. Familiarity with a species does not establish every population’s survival, habitat, or behavior in our chosen era.

| Familiar species | Proposed place and frequency | What the player actually encounters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turtle duck | Common at selected sheltered ponds; first in Ba Dan | Paddling, preening, climbing out of water, following one another through a gap. One individual grows comfortable around Dorin; others remain wary. |
| Ostrich horse | Familiar working animal on coast and inland roads | A handler checking tack, an animal refusing an unfamiliar ferry ramp, a rider making room for a tired mount. Optional supervised interaction, not automatic ownership. |
| Flying lemur | Occasional at Bellweather and nearby wooded terraces | A small group exploring hanging cloth, grooming, and escaping into inaccessible roosts. No replacement Momo or obligatory permanent mascot. |
| Flying / sky bison | Occasional visitors at Bellweather; not a herd in every village | A broad moving shadow, a landing in an appropriate clearing, grooming with a handler, quiet breathing while people wait nearby. |
| Badgermole | Rarely seen beneath the mineral hills | Tracks and displaced earth before a sighting. Its digging affects where people can safely work; a respectful encounter is more memorable than a combat spawn. |
| Hog monkey | Localized groups in wooded upland routes | Rustling branches, grooming, disputes over a dropped cloth, retreat into the canopy. Secure a camp's belongings rather than chase every animal for a reward. |
| Shirshu | A rare, supervised visitor with a professional tracker | A visibly unusual animal at rest with its handler. No petting prompt. Any future tracking story must respect the species' abilities and limits; it cannot magically locate the whole plot. |

**Parcel is now an ostrich horse**, consistently with Bible revision 1.1. The earlier mossback otter-goat, kitefin skimmer, lantern beetle, and clapper-goose designs are retired from active use. The Bible now uses turtle ducks in SQ-04, an ordinary goose without mimicry in SQ-06, a river fish in SQ-07, Parcel in SQ-16, and fireflies in EV-14. These changes remove invented taxonomic claims while preserving the stories’ functions.

**No living lion turtle is staged in this campaign.** Historical images or discussion can refer to established lore. Aang’s encounter does not establish a new post-Korra individual or local population, and this project does not invent an extinction date. SQ-35 is retired rather than quietly repurposed.

Do not populate one temperate watershed with every creature from every climate. A later polar journey can introduce appropriate wildlife there. Rare animals need a credible habitat or reason to visit. Existing characters' age and the post-Korra era do not establish the survival or location of any specific canon animal.

### Give animals readable lives

Write a small behavioral vocabulary for each important creature: resting, feeding or foraging, moving, social behavior, and a clear response to someone approaching too closely. The player learns by watching. A quiet approach can produce a longer observation; it does not make every wild animal friendly.

Give named animals specific preferences instead of human dialogue. Parcel prefers a particular shaded stop. Dorin's turtle duck settles beside a sun-warmed board. The sky bison Brindle prefers to see its handler before anyone approaches. These are individual behaviors authored for the game, not universal franchise biology.

The ecological storytelling should remain specific. A family of turtle ducks can leave a busy pond for quieter reeds even when the water is healthy. An empty burrow can mean the animal moved. Most wildlife changes should not be evidence of Veyra's scheme. When a machine has caused harm, establish that particular connection through observations.

### Three scales of animal encounter

**Ordinary company:** watch, greet a handler, pass at a sensible distance, sit nearby. Most encounters end here and are complete experiences.

**A local complication:** an animal has a habit that conflicts with a human plan. The player observes, adjusts an object or route, and chooses an arrangement. Avoid repeating the displaced-nest plot for every species.

**A rare wonder:** a badgermole emerging in a shaft of daylight or the shadow of a sky bison crossing the valley. Allow sound, scale, and silence to carry the moment. A creature's value does not depend on becoming recruitable, rideable, fightable, or collectible.

### Four supporting roles to develop

**NPC-21 — Pemi, she/her, 44, non-bender.** Name for the previously unnamed proprietor of the Bent Ladle, not a second lunch boat. Strong arms, a patched apron, an unnecessarily magnificent serving spoon. She describes a cramped vessel as if it were a grand dining establishment. Her broth deserves the boasting. She remembers how people like their food, welcomes affection, and tires of unsolicited advice. She wants to take the boat somewhere for pleasure after years of navigating only between work berths. Her adult daughter lives upriver; a visit is an invitation, not a missing-person story. Pemi and Tavi know one another well enough to disagree in half-sentences. Give her relaxed, ordinary replies as often as grand announcements.

**NPC-22 — Haro, he/him, 36, non-bender.** Name and recurring role for the manager of SQ-12's existing street troupe. He makes excellent puppets and dreadful heroic speeches. His company includes several competent performers whose preferences matter. He genuinely enjoys seeing strangers laugh together. He wants to stage a production in which nobody is secretly royalty, cursed, or the owner of a magical sword; unfortunately, he has written all three into the opening scene. His deeper arc concerns listening to collaborators and finding his own voice, separate from the existing need to distinguish fiction from the party's actual history. Riko can love his mysteries without Haro becoming her moral instructor. Seli remains the separate movement coach in Riko's personal quest.

**NPC-23 — Dalu, he/him, 63, non-bender.** Name for SQ-25's previously unnamed exhausted marsh worker. He repairs nets, fishes when he feels like it, and draws birds in the wrong proportions. His observations are better than his drawings. He knows which bank catches the last warmth, which conversation needs silence, and when he has had enough visitors. He likes long stories told badly. He has a grown son in Causeway with whom he gets along; family contact need not conceal estrangement. His later invitation to see the city is motivated by curiosity. He is allowed to enjoy it and still prefer coming home.

**NPC-24 — Viri, they/them, 33, non-bender.** A traveling animal keeper staying at Bellweather with a sky bison called Brindle and its transport crew. They belong to the wider community supporting Air traditions but are not a spokesperson for all Air culture. Viri has a practical, amused relationship with an animal vastly larger than they are. They tell novices what to watch for, admit when Brindle's behavior puzzles them, and decline risky requests plainly. Their private passion is a tiny pressed-flower collection; they frequently remember a meadow more clearly than a spectacular aerial view. They want to teach another willing keeper well enough to take an afternoon off. Brindle remains an individual under established care, not a quest reward.

## 5. Turn existing side stories into ongoing relationships

The original missions retain their IDs, entry conditions, and resolutions. These are optional follow-up beats attached to those stories. They do not reset completed quests or require every earlier visit before a friendly conversation is possible. A later introduction can acknowledge that the party missed an earlier event.

### SQ-02 continuation — Dorin after the gate

The original backward gate and incorrect register remain intact. On the next suitable visit, Dorin asks the party to look at his miniature gate because they are now people he can show an imperfect piece of work to. He does not need help with another official mistake.

On a later visit, the party finds him sitting beside a turtle duck that has taken to his sunny board. He leaves space without announcing an exemption. Pella considers this proof that he has recruited an assistant. SQ-31 can grow out of this relationship, but the animal can be introduced independently if SQ-02 was skipped.

After a constructive resolution of SQ-02, he remains willing to admit errors publicly. If the party mocked him, he is more guarded, yet his life still moves forward. The duck's affection is not a prize the player can withhold. During a campaign emergency, the existing evacuation callback takes priority; a miniature-float discussion waits.

### SQ-16 continuation — Parcel's ordinary route

Keep the eaten labels, external parcel clues, and choice between retraining and a second handler. A later meeting takes place at a rest stop, with deliveries already complete. The handler knows which packages Parcel tolerates and which smells require an additional wrapping. The party can watch the untacking, help under direction, or chat.

At another stop, Parcel interrupts an impressive account of the day's work by laying its head in an empty feed basket. The handler wants to attend Orchard Wind Day; the question is who can cover one already-familiar portion of the route. The party can participate, help arrange a willing substitute, or simply wish them a good festival. No indefinite pet-care obligation follows.

If the original route remains unresolved, picture labels or shared handling can be offered again with the specific problem acknowledged. If Parcel previously resisted the party, patience can rebuild comfort; it does not switch instantly to adoration after one line. A good later scene ends with Parcel recognizing the rest stop, not with a summoning ability.

### SQ-12 continuation — A troupe with a life beyond the heroes

After correcting the quarry account, return to find Haro rehearsing an entirely different story. He asks the party to play a door. The difficulty is artistic: the door is supposed to be intimidating without saying anything.

The next visit reveals a collaborative disagreement. One performer wants to try a quieter scene; Haro worries an audience will leave if nobody falls over. The player can watch both short rehearsals, join as an extra, or encourage a mixed program. This is about artistic taste and trust, not identifying a villain or publicly disciplining someone. Both versions can be entertaining.

Later, the troupe visits Copper Orchard. A familiar face recognizes the party even when they are far from Causeway. Riko's personal story with Seli is handled separately and receives space if active; the light rehearsal does not overwrite it. The troupe's final performance can include an optional reference to the party's participation, then continue with material about other people.

### SQ-10 continuation — Latch comes to dinner

If Latch was welcomed and a clear offering arrangement exists, they return with a carefully chosen object they have permission to give. It is a perfectly serviceable pebble. The host accepts it; nothing goes wrong.

A second social visit lets the player choose where to sit while Latch rearranges permitted table decorations. They are trying to make the human gathering comfortable according to an unfamiliar aesthetic. The player may ask about their choices, offer a preference, or leave the arrangement alone. A funny difference need not be turned into an argument about property again.

Later, Latch can invite the party to see a little grouping of objects at a marsh threshold. No invitation is compulsory and no spirit bargain is hidden inside it. If the party declined hosting earlier, the invitation can originate from a willing host the player later meets. An unintroduced or unwelcome Latch never appears at the party's table by default.

### SQ-22 continuation — The frogs have another engagement

Once the original stage problem is resolved, the musician announces a second concert. This time the frogs are naturally quiet. There is no sabotage, damaged habitat, or missing spirit.

The party helps choose an enjoyable alternative: Fenn's puppet interlude, an audience rhythm game using ordinary instruments, or a quiet listening walk. None requires a music minigame; an authored sequence and a few choices convey participation. The musician can discover that a disappointing rehearsal does not mean the evening is ruined.

A later visit catches the frogs calling when there is no concert. The musician is there alone, listening without trying to conduct. Let this be a small image rather than an explanation of what he learned. In another scene he can still be hilariously theatrical.

### SQ-25 continuation — Dalu goes somewhere

Keep the first fishing trip exactly as restful as promised. Nothing attacks, nothing important is hidden in the catch, and Dalu's request not to solve anything is honored.

On a later visit he asks what the party liked in Causeway. He is considering a visit and wants a personal impression, not a report about the network. The player can recommend the lamps, the kitchen, a performance, or an ordinary riverside walk. This can remember activities the player actually experienced; a generic suggestion remains available.

After safe travel is available, Dalu can be encountered enjoying his chosen outing with his son. He has navigated the trip himself. In the marsh afterward, he has one new story and an appalling drawing of a bridge. His happiness is not dependent on the party becoming his only friend.

## 6. Four active new stories and one retired proposal

These new stories add different kinds of experience. Each has a complete local outcome, a composition-neutral route, and room for a later visit. Specialist moments are optional and never confer an unearned discipline.

### SQ-31 — An Officer and a Turtle Duck

**Place:** Ba Dan's proposed spring pond and garden edge. **People:** Dorin, Pella, a local gardener. **First feeling:** affectionate absurdity. **Underlying question:** Can you care for something without arranging its whole life around you?

**First visit — an unofficial assistant.** A turtle duck rests beside Dorin's inspection board. Pella calls it Deputy. Dorin objects to the title, then accidentally uses it. The party can watch it paddle, ask about it, or continue toward the main road. The animal does not have a problem to solve. Introduce it before asking the player to care about a later change.

**Second visit — the wrong improvement.** After the quarry rescue, Dorin proudly shows a little shelter beside his post. Deputy ignores it and joins the other turtle ducks beneath a garden footbridge. His attempt at care was sincere, but the shelter is hot and isolated from the flock. The gardener knows where the ducks usually rest. Pella wants to carry Deputy back; Dorin hesitates.

**Player activity:** compare the shaded bank and exposed board, watch where the flock moves, and speak to the gardener. Help move the empty shelter to an appropriate place near the pond or repurpose it for garden tools while leaving the natural shelter alone. Neither requires bending. Do not pick up, chase, bait, or trap the duck to make the scene resolve.

**Choice and outcome:** Dorin may maintain a useful pond-side shelter, or accept that the flock already has one and spend his effort clearing a safe garden passage. Both respect the animal's behavior and let him keep caring. If the party encourages him to insist on the post, he tries once, observes the duck leave, and can reconsider later. No animal is harmed to punish a dialogue choice.

**Later visit:** Deputy may still rest beside the board on a cool morning. Dorin has stopped treating that as a duty roster. Pella draws the duck at the pond as well as at the gate: it has a life elsewhere. A miniature duck appears on Dorin's parade float.

**Opening-loss variant:** the first pleasant encounter remains available, but the shelter project waits until liberation. Dorin has work to do and Pella is still waiting for Bo-shan. Never replace the missing-person acknowledgment with a triumphant animal celebration.

**Optional Kaya:** offers a beautifully braided little hanging decoration for the empty shelter, if the gardener confirms it will stay clear of the animals. Her contribution is a known personal skill. **Neutral route:** anyone can help carry the shelter with Dorin.

### SQ-32 — Beneath the Useful Road

**Place:** an unused cutting at the Copper Orchard fringe, separate from the occupied quarry. **People:** Hesh and a local path worker. **Creature:** a badgermole. **First feeling:** curiosity becoming awe. **Underlying question:** When is the most capable action to stop and understand?

**Hook:** Hesh has placed a bench in a place with no view. Every so often he puts his tea down and rests a hand on the stone. Ask, and he explains that something large has been moving below the unused cutting. He is checking a path, not hiding an ancient temple.

**Investigation:** inspect fresh earth, a moved drainage stone, and two surface openings while remaining outside the unstable area. The worker's sketches show that filling each opening has merely sent the animal farther along. Its passage is not a coded message and it is not tunneling toward a secret weapon.

**Action:** accompany a small survey from stable overlooks. A collapse of loose spoil blocks the planned return path; the retreat ledge remains safe. Clear a modest surface obstruction with tools and help, or follow the marked longer way around. Earthbending may help where the ground is verified stable; blasting into the burrow makes no sense and Hesh says why. No rescue of trapped children is added.

**Reveal:** the badgermole emerges across the cutting, shifts a mass of earth with effortless control, pauses, and continues. It is an animal moving through its home. Use distance and scale. There is no musical taming sequence, romance cave, or lost earthbending lesson to reproduce.

**Choice:** make a longer permanent surface detour or close this optional spur while the animal uses it, with a clear alternative route. The local path crew accepts responsibility for the chosen arrangement. The party does not have to return repeatedly to perform maintenance. Hesh can admire both the animal's ability and the workers' quieter solution.

**Return:** fresh tracks are visible below the viewpoint; the sign reflects the route actually chosen. A safe later observation may show the animal again, but absence is also natural. If the quest is declined or abandoned, the crew closes the suspect spur and posts the longer path. No main route or clue is lost.

**Knowledge boundary:** nothing here proves the vane mechanism or Veyra's informed refusal. **Personal boundary:** Lin Mei's participation may express curiosity; it does not complete her separate accountability quest.

### SQ-33 — Six Feet, No Hurry

**Place:** Bellweather landing meadow and sheltered terrace. **People:** Viri and Sena. **Creature:** Brindle, a visiting sky bison. **First feeling:** delight at enormous ordinary behavior. **Underlying question:** Can a shared experience be worthwhile when it does not happen as planned?

**Arrival:** a shadow passes over the path. At the meadow the party finds Brindle lying down, comfortable and utterly unavailable for the dramatic departure a visiting sketch group had imagined. Viri says there will be no passenger outing today. Nobody is stranded and there is no emergency behind the delay.

**Player activity:** meet Viri, observe Brindle's signals, and choose a supervised ordinary task: carry the grooming basket, straighten a shade cloth clear of the animal, or help prepare the visitors' picnic farther from the resting space. Watching is also a complete option. Viri handles the animal; no player needs airbending or a dexterity test.

**Complication:** Oru, if physically present, has promised a grand description of the valley from above. He can instead tell a story from below; the sketch group can draw the sleeping bison. Sena proposes a picnic with a view of six exceptionally relaxed feet. The evening turns out enjoyable without the promised spectacle.

**Choice:** join the meadow gathering, help the group choose another local outing, or continue exploring. Viri appreciates help but does not charge friendship as admission to the animal. Brindle is not forced upright with bending. No one wins ownership by completing tasks.

**Later invitation:** after a separate, safe travel transition, Viri may invite the party to accompany an already-planned short journey. A brief authored overlook scene can convey it if flight gameplay is unavailable. The invitation depends on practical conditions, not perfect obedience. Declining it leaves the friendship intact. During the final crisis, ordinary travel is deferred.

**Return:** sometimes the meadow is empty, with a friendly note from Viri about the next visit. Brindle's absence is travel, not a new kidnapping plot. A later conversation concerns Viri's meadow flowers or another keeper's first successful shift.

### SQ-34 — The Bent Ladle Takes a Holiday

**Place:** Saltstep, a calm upstream berth, and the familiar lunch boat. **People:** Pemi, Tavi, and Pemi's adult daughter. **First feeling:** comic expectation. **Underlying question:** What does it feel like to let people care for the person who usually hosts everyone?

**First visit:** eat or sit at the existing Bent Ladle. Pemi changes the boat's angle to keep a pleasant view and carries on talking as the shoreline moves behind her. Establish that this is a valued place with regulars. She can ask what brought the party to town without requiring them to accept a job.

**Invitation:** on a later calm day she is going upriver to see her daughter. The daughter has invited her for lunch. Pemi has packed enough kitchen equipment to cater a wedding because she cannot picture arriving as a guest.

**Player activity:** help her choose what to bring, move a few harmless baskets, and review the ordinary trip with Tavi. Pemi can take a favorite bowl and a modest contribution rather than the entire kitchen. There is no flood, pirate attack, compulsory spending, or hidden spoiled-food emergency.

**Middle:** the boat reaches the berth. Her daughter has cooked something Pemi would have done differently. Pemi starts to give instructions, notices the table is already set for her, and has to decide how to receive the invitation. The player may suggest sharing a recipe afterward, distract her with the view, or let the family find their own rhythm. The daughter is warm and capable, with preferences of her own.

**Outcome:** a shared lunch and a plan for another visit, or an awkward but recoverable afternoon if Pemi keeps taking over. The point is a relationship becoming more reciprocal, not the party diagnosing her. Tenzo can recognize the difficulty without being assigned the only wise response.

**Later visit:** a dish inspired by the daughter's cooking appears at the Bent Ladle. Pemi names her as its source. The boat may close for a planned afternoon; a sign gives a clear return time. If the party declined the trip, Pemi can still go and later tell them about it. She does not need permission to live.

## 8. Long-term story growth

### During The Common Current: become familiar before becoming important

Act I establishes home and absence. Dorin and the pond offer a small ordinary pleasure; Pella's deeper playfulness returns gradually after Bo-shan comes home. Preserve the seriousness of the rescue.

Act II opens hospitality and curiosity. Pemi, Tavi, Haro, and Latch give the journey a social life. Some encounters concern the investigation; others are simply a meal, a performance, or an evening under the new lights. Let the player enjoy what is at stake before the network's danger becomes fully understood.

Act III gives each spoke a distinctive kind of pleasure. Copper Orchard offers craft and physical discovery; Reedglass offers listening, odd neighbors, and quiet companionship; Bellweather offers air, scale, play, and travelers. Existing personal quests deepen selected heroes. The six follow-up threads can be encountered between main missions in different orders.

Act IV acknowledges urgency and earlier choices. Familiar people may offer concrete help, disagree, or be busy protecting their own households. Do not turn every acquaintance into a recruit. Pemi feeding a few neighbors is meaningful even if she never appears in the final confrontation.

Act V keeps the existing crisis and settlement. Familiar objects and faces show why the work matters. The aftermath includes a visit for pleasure as well as public accounting. The village should be capable of hosting another ordinary afternoon.

### After the campaign: let victory create new stories

The first longer continuation should be a traveling season, not an immediate larger catastrophe. The existing final cart and optional letter already provide a natural departure.

**Proposed continuation — The Traveling Season.** A regional circuit of fairs, visiting craftspeople, performances, and ordinary family journeys connects places the player now knows. Haro's troupe wants to try a new play. Pemi wants to visit someone without bringing her job. Viri is training a second keeper. Jinu, if selected, has work worth doing that does not depend on opening private mail. These different wants supply invitations; the party chooses what to accompany.

The emotional throughline is belonging without possession: being welcome in a place does not mean everyone must stay there, and leaving can be responsible when people make real plans. A friend may move to pursue a trade. Another may decide that the small place they already live is enough. The party can support either choice without persuading every person to join its orbit.

Give this continuation three substantial episode proposals:

| Episode seed | Adventure and social stakes | Lasting result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Part Nobody Wanted** | A performer in Haro's troupe wants to stop playing the comic fool. Test new scenes at different stops while a difficult rehearsal, a damaged prop, and conflicting artistic ambitions threaten the performance. A physical travel obstacle supplies action; no secret tyrant finances the show. | Different roles and a changed performance, with a friendship allowed to survive disagreement. A failed show becomes a story the troupe tells later. |
| **An Invitation for One** | An adult acquaintance receives a genuine opportunity away from home. Help them inspect the place and try the work while family members express different hopes. The destination contains interesting strangers and an independent local adventure. | The acquaintance stays, leaves, or tries a limited visit by their own decision. Their relationship with the party changes location rather than ending. |
| **The Things We Carry Back** | A trip's final leg invites each participant to choose what to bring home: a learned craft, a repaired friendship, a story, or an object with a known owner. A difficult crossing calls for practical cooperation and letting someone else lead. | The next homecoming visibly includes something learned elsewhere. The party is also received by people whose lives changed during its absence. |

These are development seeds, not extra main-mission IDs or fully specified quests. Their casts, destinations, and detailed branches remain open. They provide a long-term direction without pretending the next campaign is already written.

### Preserve every campaign ending

| Existing ending | What the traveling season inherits |
| --- | --- |
| E-01 — The Shared Current | Cooperative links function with reduced capacity. Journeys can celebrate what works while allowing ordinary disagreements and remaining repairs. |
| E-02 — The Stewardship Winter | Useful central service operates under external limits. The promised review still matters and receives an explicit follow-up; cheerful travel does not silently erase it. |
| E-03 — Many Small Lights | Some journeys use slower local links and different hosts. Communities can be proud and welcoming while recovery remains uneven. The party's victory is real. |

Veyra and Ruon retain their individual custody, accountability, and relationship outcomes. A cheerful continuation does not place either at a public picnic as a beloved guest by default. Rusk does not return simply because a sequel needs a familiar villain.

### Beyond the Reach: make being a guest the next challenge

A later major adventure can follow the existing invitation to another region. The heroes now have a reputation for helping, which creates a different test: can they arrive somewhere new without assuming they know its needs?

Begin with a host's actual invitation, a local celebration or useful shared project, unfamiliar wildlife, and two or three people whose desires matter independently of the heroes. Let the party enjoy being there before asking it to take responsibility. The new conflict should have its own motives and causal chain; it should not be another public-works director concealing another unsafe machine.

The long-term world includes meaningful visits to Republic City and places within the four nations, developed in stages. A first distant arc could grow from Tenzo’s or Wen’s Republic City ties; Water Tribe, Fire Nation, Earth territory, and Air Nation journeys each need their own hosts, geography, and local life. These are destination commitments for development, not a claim that five new maps exist. Select each destination’s playable scope only when its culture, geography, and actual stories can be developed together. A polar voyage, for example, should be a distinct journey with appropriate animals and people, not a new climate placed one screen beyond Bellweather. No next-Avatar reveal, canon-relative surprise, or larger apocalypse is necessary to justify going farther.

## 9. Dialogue samples and short animation opportunities

### DLG-13 — Deputy

**When:** SQ-31's first encounter, in a safe Ba Dan scene. No claim that the quarry has already been freed.

*Dorin shifts his inspection board. A turtle duck follows the patch of sun and settles beside it.*

**Pella:** Deputy's asleep again.

**Dorin:** It doesn't work here, Pella.

*Dorin tries to place a paper on the board. The duck rests its chin on the edge.*

**Dorin:** Deputy. Move your head.

*Pella looks at him. He continues very carefully with the paper.*

**Player options:** ask how long the duck has been visiting / sit at a sensible distance / continue toward the road.

**If asked:** “Most mornings this week. It comes up from the pond when the sun reaches the board.”

The joke ends there. No one explains a lesson about bureaucracy. If the player asks about the missing people, Dorin answers seriously and the conversation changes tone.

### DLG-14 — The best table

**When:** the first or an ordinary repeat visit to the Bent Ladle; travel is safe.

**Pemi:** Take that seat. You can see the whole harbor.

*The player sits. The moored lunch boat slowly turns; the view becomes a stack of freight crates on the quay. Pemi follows the player's gaze.*

**Pemi:** Give it a minute.

*She sets down a bowl. The boat turns a little further. A narrow strip of water appears between two crates.*

*Pemi picks up her own bowl and sits beside the player, leaving the other seats empty.*

**Pemi:** I liked it better before they unloaded.

**Optional Tenzo:** How long have you had this berth?

**Pemi:** Since breakfast. I might move again.

*She asks about the party's day. The lunch is still good and she need not sell the view any harder.*

Offer a meal conversation, a view, or departure. Do not reveal an urgent errand behind the hospitality.

### DLG-15 — At the meadow

**When:** SQ-33, after Viri has explained that today's outing is postponed.

*Viri spreads an empty picnic cloth beside the resting area, clear of Brindle's feet. Brindle exhales. One corner folds neatly over the other.*

*Viri smooths it flat. They pause with a hand on the corner, watching the bison breathe. On the next exhale, the far corner flips up.*

*They lift the cloth and indicate a spot away from the draft. The party can help carry it. Brindle keeps sleeping.*

*Sena, if present, brings the picnic basket. Nobody needs to add a joke about airbenders or laziness.*

### Keep proposed behavior separate from implementation

The narrative needs remembered outcomes, contextual dialogue, visible return states, and optional observation. It does not require a continuous animal simulation, a complete friendship economy, real-time tides, procedural quests, flight controls, or NPC schedules running while the player is away.

If a behavior is not supported, express it honestly through a short authored interaction or a changed scene on return. A quest may describe a bison journey without asserting that player-controlled flight exists. A family trip can occur between visits without pretending the game simulates every intervening hour.

### Final editorial checks for the next writing pass

First check the shared Part 01 contract: era, named world anchors, technology, powers, spirit limits, animal identity, and branch history. Mark every addition as canon-supported, compatible original fiction, gameplay abstraction, or held. Then read a scene once for pleasure: does it have an image, action, voice, or discovery worth experiencing? Then check what it asks the player to do, what the characters know, and what changes afterward.

Give important decisions understandable consequences. Give small jokes permission to stay small. Let people change through several experiences instead of one correct answer. Let strength be useful, let restraint be useful, and let ordinary expertise matter. The world should offer responsibilities the player can choose to meet and pleasures they do not have to earn.

The long-term promise is a place with enough life that a player can say, “Before we go, I want to stop at the pond,” and the story understands why.
