## The five questions

### 1. The gardens, the tea terrace, the wash steps, the two houses

- **The vegetable gardens are common ground, worked in strips, one strip to a household.** Each strip is marked
  with a pale stone peg from the quarry, and each peg has its household's mark cut into it (a fish for Hanru, a
  cart wheel for Bo-shan and Nala, a scale for Gao). Nobody owns the tools; they hang in one lean-to.
  - Bo-shan and Nala's strip is the largest. It fed the quarry cookhouse **(shipped)**, and that is why the garden
    path matters to the story: the vegetables went up the east road with him.
  - **Later state:** while Bo-shan is missing, his strip is the only weeded one. The neighbours have been doing it
    and nobody has said so. After a loss it has gone to seed.
  - The gardener in the turtle-duck story (living-world expansion) is whoever's strip is nearest the water. Name
    them when that story is built.
- **The tea terrace is Nala's.** She grows and fires the village's tea on the dry-stone terrace above the square,
  which is where the brown stain on her apron comes from (Act IV). Her knee is bad, so Pella does the picking on
  the top step. The find up there should be two stools, one of them small, and the whole village laid out below.
- **The wash steps are nobody's.** By habit the houses past the canal wash at dawn and the square's side washes
  after the midday meal. The basket and board left on the steps are always there and always somebody else's. The
  line between the shrine and the guard hut is the watch's own washing **(bible)**.
- **The two houses** are Dorin's (north-east) and Hanru's (south-east), as in the table.

### 2. Hesra

Yes: **move her to the mason's yard.** She lodges in the mason's spare room **(shipped: `return.ts`; Act IV)**. A
south rim door was a stand-in. The yard's step is where her boot is left, so the step must be visible from the
lane.

The mason is **Tuo**: a woman in her sixties who has cut every doorstep in Ba Dan and says little. She is
background, not a quest giver; she needs no portrait until a scene asks for one.

### 3. Dorin's and Hanru's houses; the gate

- Houses: as above. The two guards live on either side of the road they watch, which Dorin thinks is proper and
  Hanru has never remarked on.
- **The gate is a real gate across the road, in a low wall that stops a few paces either side of it.** Carts go
  through it. People, hens and Pella walk round the end of the wall, and the ground shows it: worn round, not
  through. This is the bible's "a gate almost everyone walks around". Dorin still asks everyone to stop.

### 4. The school and the bell

- **Ba Dan has no schoolhouse.** Class is a morning under the banyan at the riverside, down the south lane
  **(shipped: the residents' data sends Pella "down the south lane", and the notice says "Pupils home at
  midday")**. Whoever Mira has talked into it takes the morning; the notice board says whose turn it is. Keep the
  school notice in the village and build nothing for it on this board. The riverside brief should leave a patch of
  worn ground and a few flat stones under the banyan.
- **The village's one bell hangs on the east gate post**, and the watch rings it **(shipped: Dorin's "I'm the one
  who rings it", Act IV)**. New: it is the quarry's old shift bell, brought down when the company left. For sixty
  years it meant stone was coming. It has not rung for a month when the game opens.

### 5. Past the west ford

- **The west road climbs to the hill farms.** Hesra came down it to learn masonry **(shipped: she is from a hill
  farm)**. It floods at the ford every spring; this year the water has not gone down.
- **It does not open in the demo, and no content is planned behind it.** Treat it as a quiet edge with room for an
  exit later. If a hill-farm story is written, the earliest it opens is the return to Ba Dan in Act IV.
- **The find at the ford:** a waymark stone at the water's edge with a hill-farm knot cut into it, and the road
  going under. It should tell the player where the road goes and that someone they will meet came down it.

Note: "the west road" in the shipped lines (`return.ts`, `act1.ts`) is the road **west from the quarry**, which
is Ba Dan's east road. It is not this one. The board should not label the ford's road "the west road".

## Small facts the brief marked as placeholders

- **Where rubbish goes:** nothing leaves. Stone chips mend the ruts in the road in, kitchen scraps go to the heap
  at the end of the gardens, and whatever is left goes into Gao's trough-side bucket for the cart animals.
- **Pella's household adult** is Nala from the start. The stand-in can carry her name once she has art.

## New here, for the owner

1. The mason is named Tuo.
2. The tea terrace is Nala's, and Pella picks for her.
3. The gardens are common strips marked with carved pegs; Bo-shan's strip is weeded by neighbours while he is gone.
4. The gate bell is the quarry's old shift bell.
5. The west road leads to the hill farms, and nothing is planned behind the ford.
6. The quarry houses are a company-built row where Leto, Amri and Senn live.
7. The school is a morning class under the riverside banyan, taught in turns.
