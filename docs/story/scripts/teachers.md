# The five teachers — scripts for TR-01 to TR-05

Luli, Aru, Hesh, Oru and Edda. Each story opens one rare discipline path for the heroes of one element. The
ten personal quests are in [personal-quests-1.md](personal-quests-1.md) and
[personal-quests-2.md](personal-quests-2.md).

Sources: the bible's Part 05 ("Discipline teacher stories"), its powers section (Part 01 §9), and the
supporting cast (Luli NPC-12, Aru NPC-14, Hesh NPC-11, Oru NPC-13, Edda NPC-15); the story bible's gap-fill 21
(Air Projection); and the act scripts, which make each teacher's offer (Scenes 3.1, 4.1 and 5.5) and call
one lesson back in the finale (Scene 13.3).

## How to read these scripts

The reading key at the top of [act2-sparks.md](act2-sparks.md) holds: one speaker per line, `Narrator` lines
as stage directions, `Party:` lines as tappable choice text, **Shown**, the **Driver**. What is particular to
a teacher story:

- **Who can take it.** Each lesson needs one hero of its element in the party: either of two. The marker
  stands only then. With both in the party, the table picks who steps up; the flag is the same either way
  and opens the path for both (the flag records access, not years of practice: bible).
- **The student's lines** are written as `Party:` choices, so that the lesson reads the same for either
  hero. Each hero then has two or three lines of their own, marked "If Kaya is the student". Nothing else in
  the party is needed, and no other element is ever asked for.
- **The teacher's "again".** Every lesson has one prompt with an unsafe or cheeky choice in it. Taking it
  never closes the lesson. The teacher stops, says why, and offers the attempt again (bible: "Teachers can
  decline unsafe behavior and offer another supervised attempt").
- **No fight is needed.** TR-01 offers one, optionally.
- **Each lesson can be found twice.** The first place is in the coast-to-city corridor during Act II, before
  any level-five choice (bible). The second is where the teacher is later. A table that meets the path late
  can still take the lesson; see "Open, for the owner", item 1, about a choice already made.

## TR-01 — A Safe Place for Thunder (Luli)

**Theme card:** TR-01, A Safe Place for Thunder

Themes: responsibility, balance. Lightning here is a trade: little careful pieces into a wire, all day, so
that somebody's grandmother can see her stairs. The first thing Luli teaches is when to stop. She has stopped
counting her own days without one, and nobody has taught the lamp-hours board the same lesson.

- [x] No nation, element or culture stereotype
- [x] Every valid party has a real route through (this lesson is optional; fire's other path needs no flag)
- [x] No instant forgiveness or absolution (not at issue)
- [x] The other side's reasons are shown (the crowd wants more light, and more light is a good thing)
- [x] The theme is never stated outright
- [x] Absent heroes are never voiced

**Where:** the Emberwatch power yard, after MQ-03. **Later:** the Causeway lamp sheds, where Luli comes for
inspections (Scene 5.5). **Needs:** Kaya or Tenzo.

> **The offer, already written (Act II, Scene 3.1):**
>
> **Luli:** You've got the hands for it. I'll teach you. Not today, and not as a dare. First lesson is when to
> stop. If you want the lesson where you show how brave you are, find somebody else.

### Scene T1.1 — Twelve lamps

**Shown:** played. **Where:** the power yard. A short practice spur runs off the live line to a string of
twelve lamps hung across the parade ground for the lantern season.

**Narrator:** The practice spur is thirty paces of wire on low posts, with a copper rod driven into the ground
at each end. Two riggers stand by the rods in rope-soled shoes. There is a gauge on a board, with a needle,
and a red mark two-thirds of the way round.

**Narrator:** On the wall behind it is another board, in chalk: "LAMP-HOURS OWED." The number under it has
been rubbed out and written larger several times.

**Luli:** That string's the parade ground's. Twelve lamps. Tonight the children here see their own beans
after dark, first time. You're going to light it. Some of it.

**Party:** How?

**Luli:** You know heat. This isn't heat. Heat, you push. This, you pull apart. There's two things in you
that sit together. You ease them away from each other, gently, like taking cream off milk.

**Luli:** And then you don't hold it. That's what kills people, the holding. You let it go where it already
wants to go, which is down that wire. You're a gate. You're not a jar.

**Narrator:** She laughs: the engine, trying to start.

**Luli:** Small. I want a thing the size of a sneeze. Hand on the post. When I say.

> **If Kaya is the student —**
> **Kaya:** The size of a sneeze. I can do bigger than a sneeze.
>
> **Luli:** I'm sure you can. I've buried a rigger who could. Sneeze.

> **If Tenzo is the student —**
> **Tenzo:** Like bringing milk to a simmer and never letting it catch.
>
> **Luli:** If that helps. Does milk kill you when it catches?
>
> **Tenzo:** Only if my grandmother's watching.

**Luli:** Riggers?

**Riggers:** Rods in! Clear!

**Luli:** Now.

**Narrator:** It is not like fire. It goes out of the student's hand cold and blue and all at once, and the
first lamp on the string comes on.

**Narrator:** A child on the parade ground shrieks. Others come running. The gauge needle has moved the
width of a thumbnail.

**Luli:** Again. Same size.

**Narrator:** The second lamp. The third. A crowd has gathered among the beans: loaders, the retired cook, a
dozen children. Each lamp gets a cheer.

**Narrator:** Four. Five. Six. The needle climbs. At seven the wire begins to hum, low, like a bee in a
jar.

**Luli:** Hear that? That's the wire telling you something. Listen to it. Don't listen to them.

**Narrator:** Eight. The needle is on the red mark. The hum has gone up a note.

**Luli:** That's eight. Eight's what this spur carries. Stop there.

**Narrator:** Four lamps are still dark. The children have noticed.

**Children:** More! Do the rest! Do all of them!

**A rigger off shift** (from the fence)**:** Go on! Bet you can't light the tower lamp from there! Bet you
a week's tea!

**PROMPT (the decider; two choices):**

- **Stop at eight.**
- **Light the other four.**

**If "Light the other four":**

**Narrator:** The student's hand goes back to the post. The ninth lamp comes on, brighter than the others,
and white.

**Narrator:** Luli throws the breaker. Every lamp on the string goes out. The crowd groans.

**Luli:** Rods!

**Riggers:** Rods in! Clear!

**Narrator:** The hum dies. The gauge needle drops back, slowly, as if it were tired.

**Luli:** I'm not angry. Look at me. I'm not. You did it clean, and it was one lamp too many, and the wire
was past its mark. Another two and it sags onto the fence, and the fence is where they're standing.

**Luli:** You heard me say stop. You heard them say more. They were louder. They'll always be louder.

**Luli:** From nothing. All eight again. That's not a punishment. That's the lesson. Riggers?

_(The lesson returns to the first lamp. The prompt is offered again at eight.)_

**If "Stop at eight":**

**Narrator:** The student takes their hand off the post.

**Children:** Awww.

**Luli:** Eight! Eight's the lot! Go and look at your beans!

**A rigger off shift:** Call that brave?

**Luli:** I call that the only student I've had this year that I'd let near my line. You can have the
tower lamp when you've paid for the tower. Go home, Dav.

**Narrator:** She turns to the two riggers at the rods.

**Luli:** Now the bit nobody claps for. It's still in the wire. Some of what you put in is sitting there. So
we don't walk away. Rods?

**Riggers:** Rods in!

**Luli:** And you say it. You're not finished until you've said it and heard it back.

**Party:** Clear?

**Riggers:** Clear!

**Narrator:** The hum stops. The needle rests on its pin. Eight lamps burn steadily over the parade ground,
and under them a boy is counting red beans out loud.

> **If Kaya is the student —**
> **Kaya:** I wanted the other four.
>
> **Luli:** Of course you did. So do I, every night. There's forty kitchens over the water that want four
> more as well.
>
> **Kaya:** So how do you stop?
>
> **Luli:** Badly. Ask the board.

> **If Tenzo is the student —**
> **Tenzo:** When was your last day off?
>
> **Luli:** I've stopped counting. The board counts.
>
> **Tenzo:** Who tells you when to stop?
>
> **Narrator:** Luli looks at the chalk board on the wall for a moment.
>
> **Luli:** That does. It's never said it yet.

**Narrator:** Luli sits down on an upturned cable drum. It is the first time anyone has seen her sit.

**Luli:** Go on, then. The spur wants one sneeze every little while to hold eight. You know the size. I'm
going to shut my eyes until the riggers change. If the wire hums, you stop, and you wake me.

**Narrator:** The riggers stay at their rods. The student keeps eight lamps lit for an hour. The wire does not
hum. Luli does not wake. At the change of shift, one of the riggers puts his jacket over her.

_Sets `learned_lightning`. Fire heroes may now choose the lightning path at level five; the power itself
comes through the ordinary levels._

## TR-02 — Ask Before You Heal (Aru)

**Theme card:** TR-02, Ask Before You Heal

Themes: equality, respect for each other's differences. A man wants his back to stop hurting. He does not
want the treatment a healer would choose for him, and he has a reason. The lesson is that his "no" is a
part of the treatment, and that willow bark is medicine.

- [x] No nation, element or culture stereotype
- [x] Every valid party has a real route through (optional; water's other path needs no flag)
- [x] No instant forgiveness or absolution (if PQ-03 went hard, Aru teaches anyway and stays cool)
- [x] The other side's reasons are shown (the patient's refusal is sensible)
- [x] The theme is never stated outright
- [x] Absent heroes are never voiced

**Where:** the Northern healing house's outreach room at Saltstep, mornings, after MQ-03. **Later:** Aru's
folding table in Causeway (Scene 5.5). No polar travel. **Needs:** Nilak or Sura. It does not depend on
PQ-03, and no outcome of PQ-03 closes it.

> **The offer, already written (Act II, Scene 5.5):**
>
> **Aru:** We see anyone who comes to the table. I'm supposed to add "with residency papers". I've stopped
> adding it out loud. If you want to learn, the first thing I teach is asking before you touch.

### Scene T2.1 — Good morning

**Shown:** played. **Where:** the outreach room: one bench, one basin, a shelf of jars, a kettle.

**Narrator:** The outreach room is a net store with a whitewashed wall. There is a kettle going. A big man
of sixty is sitting on the bench, very upright, the way people sit when leaning hurts.

**Aru:** This is Bem. Dock porter. Bem, this one's learning. They'll do the talking and I'll watch. If you
don't like anything, you say, and it stops. That's the rule in here. It's the only one I wrote myself.

**Bem:** I've done my back. Lifting a crate that said "light". I want it to stop hurting. I've a barge to
load tomorrow.

**Aru:** There. That's all you've been told. Don't touch him. Look first. Tell me what you see.

**Party:** He's sitting to the left. He's keeping the right side still.

**Aru:** Good. What else?

**Party:** He hasn't taken his coat off. He's not sure of us.

**Aru:** Better. Now ask him something. Not about his back.

**Party:** Bem. What do you want to be able to do tomorrow?

**Bem:** Lift. Sixty crates. And get my boots on by myself, which this morning I couldn't.

> **If Nilak is the student —**
> **Nilak:** I've done this for years, Aru. I can tell you which muscle it is from the door.
>
> **Aru:** I know you can. You always could. You were the best in our year at knowing. You'd say "may I?" when
> you had already decided what.
>
> **Nilak:** ...Good morning, Bem. I'm Nilak. May I sit down?

> **If Sura is the student —**
> **Sura:** It's the long muscle on the right. I've seen it on every net-hauler at home. I can have that
> loose in the time it takes to...
>
> **Aru:** Did he ask you what it was?
>
> **Sura:** No.
>
> **Aru:** What did he ask for?
>
> **Sura:** ...His boots. Bem. Tell me about the boots.

**Aru:** Now tell him what you could do. All of it. In words he'd use.

**Party:** There's a deep treatment. Water, worked along the spine. It's the quickest. You'd feel it loosen
today.

**Bem:** No.

**Narrator:** He says it at once, and pulls his coat a little closer.

**Bem:** Not in my back. I had that done once, at a company surgery. Couldn't feel my feet till the next
evening. Maybe it was done right. I've still a gangplank to walk at six tomorrow with a crate on my shoulder.

**Narrator:** Aru says nothing. Aru is watching the student's hands.

**PROMPT (the decider; three choices):**

- **"What would you be willing to try?"**
- **"It won't be like that this time. Let me show you."**
- **"Then there's nothing I can do."**

**If "It won't be like that this time. Let me show you.":**

**Narrator:** The student's hand lifts, with water already gathering round the fingers. Aru's hand closes
on the wrist, not hard.

**Aru:** No. He said no. That was the end of that sentence.

**Aru:** You might be right. It might be nothing like the last time. He still has to walk a plank tomorrow
on feet he trusts, and he's the one who knows which feet those are.

**Aru:** Sit down. Start again. Start from "Good morning."

_(The scene returns to Bem's first line. The prompt is offered again.)_

**If "Then there's nothing I can do.":**

**Aru:** Isn't there? Look at my shelf.

**Narrator:** The shelf holds willow bark, a jar of warming salve, clean flannel, and a kettle that has
just boiled.

**Aru:** Half of what's wrong with people who walk in here, I never bend a drop for. He's refused one
thing. He's still sitting on your bench. Ask him the other question.

_(The prompt is offered again.)_

**If "What would you be willing to try?":**

**Bem:** Heat. My mother did heat. A hot cloth and something bitter to drink.

**Party:** Willow bark tea for the pain. A hot flannel on the right side, a count of two hundred. And if
you'll let me, a little cooling on the shoulder only. Nowhere near the spine. It stops when you say.

**Bem:** The shoulder. Not the back.

**Party:** Not the back.

**Narrator:** Bem thinks it over, slowly, as he would think over a load. Then he takes his coat off.

**Narrator:** The tea is bitter. The flannel is hot. The student works a hand's width of cool water into
the top of the shoulder and nowhere else, and says what they are doing before each thing.

**Bem:** That's eased.

**Party:** It'll be sore again tonight. Flannel again before you sleep. And two people on any crate that
says "light".

**Narrator:** Bem bends, carefully, and reaches his own boot. He gets one finger through the pull.

**Bem:** Huh.

**Narrator:** He gets it on by himself. It takes a while. Nobody helps.

**Aru:** You bent about a cupful.

**Party:** Was that enough?

**Aru:** Ask his boot.

> **If Nilak is the student —**
> **Nilak:** I could have had him lifting today. With the deep work.
>
> **Aru:** You could. And tomorrow?
>
> **Nilak:** ...He'd not have come back. And he'd have told nine porters not to.
>
> **Aru:** Ten. Bem talks.

> **If Sura is the student —**
> **Sura:** I'd have just done it. At home you just do it. Somebody's cold, you warm them, you don't hold a
> meeting.
>
> **Aru:** At home they know you. Bem met you a quarter of an hour ago.
>
> **Sura:** So I ask until they know me.
>
> **Aru:** You ask after that as well. But yes.

> **If `pq03_published` —**
> **Narrator:** Aru is as careful a teacher as ever. They do not offer the student tea afterwards.

**Aru:** Come back any morning. There's a great deal more. Most of it's this again, with different people.

## TR-03 — The Earth in the Hinge (Hesh)

**Theme card:** TR-03, The Earth in the Hinge

Themes: equality, responsibility. A lift gate has a bent hinge. It could be forced. Hesh would rather it
were understood, and the person who understands it best turns out to be the porter who opens it forty times
a day. Hesh knows what that means about who has been allowed to learn this. He does not say so.

- [x] No nation, element or culture stereotype
- [x] Every valid party has a real route through (optional; earth's other path needs no flag)
- [x] No instant forgiveness or absolution (not at issue here; Hesh's own admission is Scene 6.4's)
- [x] The other side's reasons are shown (forcing it would work, once)
- [x] The theme is never stated outright
- [x] Absent heroes are never voiced

**Where:** the public goods lift at the Saltstep rail annex, after MQ-03, when Hesh is visiting. **Later:**
the braking yard at Copper Orchard, where the gate is the tool store's and Nanu carries pears; her lines hold.
**Needs:** Bo or Lin Mei.

> **The offer, already written (Act II, Scene 5.5):**
>
> **Hesh:** There's a bent hinge on the public lift by the annex. You could force it. Most would. Come and
> look at it with me first. Bring whoever uses the lift.

### Scene T3.1 — Whoever uses the lift

**Shown:** played. **Where:** the lift at the rail annex: an iron cage on a chain, with a gate that hangs a
finger out of true.

**Narrator:** Hesh is standing in front of the lift gate with his hands behind his back. He has brought a
hammer, a file and a flask of tea, and has not touched any of them.

**Narrator:** Beside him is a fish porter with a yoke across her shoulders, and a girl of about twelve with
a smaller yoke.

**Hesh:** This is Nanu. She's through that gate forty times a day. That's her daughter. I said bring who
uses it. They're who uses it.

**Nanu:** It drags. You have to lift it and shove. With two baskets on.

**Hesh:** Top hinge. Bent pin. Go on. What do you do?

> **If Bo is the student —**
> **Bo:** Hold the gate, set my feet, and bring the pin back straight. It's a thumb of iron.
>
> **Hesh:** That's what you'd do. I asked what you do first.
>
> **Bo:** ...Look at it.
>
> **Hesh:** Hm.

> **If Lin Mei is the student —**
> **Lin Mei:** I would measure the deflection, then the gate's hang, and I would want to know the age of
> the pin and whether...
>
> **Hesh:** In a sentence.
>
> **Lin Mei:** I would look at it. For some time.
>
> **Hesh:** Hm.

**Narrator:** His eyebrows go up a very little. From Hesh, this is applause.

**Hesh:** Hand on the pin. Don't push. There's earth in iron. Not much. Grit that never burned out of the
ore. That's what you're feeling for. It's all you've got to hold.

**Narrator:** The student puts a hand on the hinge pin. It is cold, and for a while it is only cold.

**Narrator:** Then there is something. A faint grain, like sand in a loaf. It runs up the pin from the
bottom, and then, near the top, it stops.

**Party:** There's a place I can't feel. Near the top. It just goes blank.

**Hesh:** Does it.

**Party:** Is that where it's bent?

**Hesh:** I don't know. I can't feel it from here. Ask somebody who knows the gate.

**Narrator:** The student looks at Nanu. Nanu shrugs under her yoke.

**Nanu:** I don't know about pins. I know it's worse on a cold morning. And it squeals. Not all the way.
Just when the gate's a third open. Third bar.

**Nanu's daughter:** It didn't used to. It started after the man came with the torch.

**Hesh:** What man?

**Nanu's daughter:** Last winter. He mended it. He had a little torch and a stick of yellow metal, and he
went round the top of the pin with it. It was pretty. It dragged ever since.

**Narrator:** Hesh's eyebrows come down. He takes his hands from behind his back for the first time.

**Hesh:** Brazed. Somebody's put a collar of brass on a cracked pin. That's your blank place. There's no
earth in good brass. You can't feel it, and you can't move it. Nobody can.

**Hesh:** So. The iron below it will go where you tell it. The brass won't. Now what do you do?

**PROMPT (the decider; two choices):**

- **Force the pin straight.**
- **Ask what happens at the join.**

**If "Force the pin straight":**

**Narrator:** The student sets their feet. Hesh puts his own hand flat on the gate.

**Hesh:** Wait. You'll do it. I've no doubt. The iron comes straight, the brass stays where it is, and the
pin goes in two at the collar. Then the gate's on the floor.

**Hesh:** And thirty porters carry fish up the long stair until the foundry's got a free day. That's a
fortnight, this month. I asked.

**Hesh:** Nobody's asking you to be weaker. Hand back on the pin. Start below the blank place. Tell me where
the grain ends.

_(The prompt is offered again.)_

**If "Ask what happens at the join":**

**Hesh:** It breaks, is what happens. If you take the bend out all at once. So you don't. You take a hair
out of it, below the collar, where it's yours to move. Only a hair. Then you stop, and I do the rest the
slow way.

**Narrator:** The student finds the grain again and follows it up to where it ends. Just below that, they
lean on it. Not with a shoulder. With attention.

**Narrator:** The pin moves less than the thickness of a fingernail. The gate, which hung a finger out of
true, hangs half a finger out.

**Hesh:** Stop.

**Narrator:** The student stops.

**Hesh:** That's it. That's all the bending there is in this job.

**Narrator:** He takes the file out of his coat. He files the brass collar down, a few strokes at a time,
trying the gate between each. It takes a quarter of an hour. Then he taps the pin once with the hammer, and
the gate swings shut by itself and stays there.

**Hesh:** You got it a hair. The file got it the rest. Don't you ever look down on a file.

> **If Bo is the student —**
> **Bo:** I'd have had it in two.
>
> **Hesh:** You would.
>
> **Bo:** I didn't ask her. I'd have stood here all day feeling for it, and she knew.
>
> **Hesh:** She told you the third bar. You couldn't have got that out of the iron in a year.

> **If Lin Mei is the student —**
> **Lin Mei:** I could not have said where it would break. Only that there was a place I could not read.
>
> **Hesh:** That's worth saying. Most won't. They'd sooner guess than say "there's a place I can't read".
>
> **Lin Mei:** I have said it in reports. I usually put it at the bottom, in small letters.
>
> **Hesh:** Put it at the top.

## TR-04 — The Note You Leave Out (Oru)

**Theme card:** TR-04, The Note You Leave Out

Themes: equality, respect for each other's differences. A new weather bell is very loud. Loud turns out to
be a different thing from understood. The lesson is to put a small push of air exactly where it is wanted,
to leave a silence in the middle of a warning so that people can tell it from noise, and to hang something
beside the bell for the people who will never hear it.

- [x] No nation, element or culture stereotype (Oru is a bell tuner and a ham; nothing here is "Air
