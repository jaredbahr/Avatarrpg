## The river road

### What the road was before

- **It is the second half of the stone road.** For sixty years Stoneback's stone came down Ba Dan's east road to
  the coast **(bible)**, and Saltstep still calls Ba Dan "up the stone road" **(script, Scene 2.2)**. From the
  coast the stone went on up this road to build Causeway. The river road is older than the Works and older than
  the trams; it is as wide as it is because two stone carts had to pass.
- **Its milestones are Stoneback stone:** pale limestone, the same as every doorstep in Ba Dan and as the
  milestone on the Forest Road **(shipped: the Forest Road has one)**. Each has a number and nothing else. This
  answers the brief's `PLACEHOLDER (owner)` for the road's lasting mark, if the owner wants it to.
- **The river has no name on the road.** The people who live beside it say "the river". See the questions at the
  end.

### Who is on it

**Nuo.** Her mother built the landing; Nuo replaces one plank a year, so by now none of it is the landing her
mother built. Her boat is too small for the Ferrymen's Compact to license **(bible: the Compact's captains protect
their own licences)**, which suits her and suits them. She takes a coin or an egg for a crossing. Every morning
since the pole went down she has left two fish on the flat stone by the guards' pot, and has told nobody. She is
dry, unhurried and not a quest giver; she needs no portrait until a scene asks for one.

**The corporal's six** (facts behind the script's lines; nothing here contradicts them):

- They are provincial guards from another quarry on the supply-control list **(script)**. The quarry is not named.
- They were told to hold their gate until they were relieved. Nobody relieved them and nobody paid them. After two
  months **(script)** the corporal walked them down to the river road, because the pay cart would have to come up
  it.
- **The pole is their own gate bar.** They carried it down on two shoulders. This answers the brief's open
  question 3 from the story's side: it is a quarry gate's bar, so red and white, chipped, with the iron hoop of
  its hinge still on one end. Holding a gate is the only job anyone has given them.
- **They took their coats off first.** The corporal will not have it done in uniform. Seven grey coats are folded
  on a dry rock across the stream (the far-bank find). The buttons have been cut off and sold.
- **Why the party cannot simply pay:** the sacks on the cart are deliveries, entered in the delivery book
  **(script: "Three stops left in the book")**. The driver does not give away another household's cabbages; if
  the fight is lost, they are taken.
- **The camp's sign** (a kit piece already): one board, in careful capitals. `TOLL. ONE SACK A CART. FOOD ONLY.`
  Under it, `BY ORDER`, with a line through it.

### The bridge toll: what is in the game now

`sf23_choice`, `sf23_not_now`, `sf23_pole_up`, `enc_sf23_bridge_toll` and `river_road_sign`. The corporal's first
line, his win line and the drivers' lines are the script's and are unchanged.

**Narrator** (leaving Emberwatch, `river_road_sign`): The cart turns its back on the bay. Ahead, the road finds the
river and keeps beside it. Causeway is a day upstream.

**Narrator:** Parcel walks like an ostrich horse who has already picked the place where he is going to stop.

**Corporal** **(script)**: One sack off every cart. I'm not proud of it. I've got six who haven't eaten hot since
the last moon, and I'm the one they look at.

**Corporal** (the choice's prompt): That's the toll. A sack by the fire and the pole goes up. Don't make me think
of something worse. I'm no good at it.

- **Party:** These sacks are spoken for. Lift the pole. _A fight. His guards hold the bridge. The stream is
  shallow at the ford, a little way downstream._
- **Party:** Not yet. _No fight. The pole stays down and the corporal stays by his fire. You can come back._

**Corporal** (if "Not yet"): Then we'll be here. That's not a threat. I just can't think where else we'd be.

**Fight intro:** A pole lies across the plank bridge. Two guards stand behind it, as straight as guards are taught
to stand. In the camp at their backs, a pot hangs over a cold fire.

**Narrator** (win) **(script)**: The pole comes up.

**Narrator** (win; new): A carter who has waited on the far bank all morning drives across. She slows beside the
corporal and tells him where, in Causeway, a shelf of food is kept hot past midnight.

**Corporal** **(script)**: Where did you say? A kitchen with a late shelf? ...We'll walk it. We'll keep our hands
where the cook can see them.

Why the carter: the corporal's line answers somebody who has just told him about the Six-Ladle's late shelf. A
party on its first trip up the road has never been to Causeway, and the shelf is new this year **(script, Scene
4.1)**, so neither driver could know it. The carter comes from Causeway, so the line is true for every table.

**Children under the bell.** **Shown:** found.

**Child:** Three and a gap. Three and a gap. That means off the planks and up the steps. Even if you're winning.

**Smaller child:** We're not allowed to ring it. So we clap it. I'm the best at clapping it.

> **A second visit —**
> **Old woman:** The gap comes after four.
>
> **Child:** After three. We do it every day. You do it never.
>
> **Old woman:** ...It might be three.

**At the fish racks and the freight side.**

- **Net-mender:** Hold this. No, the other end. Good. You're good at that. Stay there till noon.

- **Fisher, sorting:** Small ones go back. Middling ones go to the bowl stall. The big one goes to whoever my
  mother's cross with, to make it up.

- **Dock worker, carrying a crate:** Mind your backs! ...It's empty. I say it anyway, or nobody moves.

**The washing gap** (a find).

**Narrator:** One washing line, from the house to the net store. One end is faded and patched. The other end has
more small shirts on it than seems possible. Underneath, the ground is worn smooth.

**The gulls' post** (a find; its journal note can be the first line).

**Narrator:** A post with no paint left on it, and gulls along the top. The weed on it stops a hand higher than
the top band on the new poles.

> **If the party waits —**
> **Narrator:** The gulls go, all at once. A moment later the bell rings, one stroke. Along the platforms, people
> bend to their mooring ropes.

> **If Sura is in the party —**
> **Sura:** That weed's above their top band. So the water's been up there. ...Who do you ask, here, about a thing
> like that?

## What pays off, and what is planted

- **From the demo:** Stoneback's stone, at the far end of the road it travelled; unpaid guards holding a pole,
  as Ruon's held a gate, without anybody saying so; the drivers' own voices (Bo-shan's boasting, Dorin's log).
- **For later:** the still water and "a year and a half" (the vane trials; Act III's audience counts the river
  road's ring); the lookout's three names (the vote in Scene 5.6); Parcel's third stop (Scene 9.8, where the
  Marshal's people cannot move him); the corporal's six on the road to the late shelf (PQ-02's kitchen, where
  unpaid work is the whole question).

## Questions only the owner can answer

1. **Does the river get a name?** Recommended: no. The people on it say "the river", and the saga's named water is
   the Works'. A name can still be put on a map later.
2. **May the party know about the late shelf before it has been to Causeway?** Recommended: no, so the carter says
   it (item 4). Deleting her line restores the script exactly.
3. **Should paying the toll be a third option?** Recommended: no. The script has two outcomes, and "the sacks are
   spoken for" is why. If the owner wants it, it is one more option and one more node, with a line from the driver
   about whose cabbages they were.
4. **Is the pole red and white?** Recommended: yes, as their own gate bar (item 3). It also settles the brief's
   open question 3 for the art.
