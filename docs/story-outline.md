# Four Nations Tactics: Echoes of Stillwater — saga outline

**Status: approved by the owner, 2026-11-14,** with this title, all 24 decisions, the beat outline and the
writing standard, and recorded in [story-bible.md](story-bible.md#owner-decisions-2026-11-14-saga-outline). This
page sharpens the approved canon ([`docs/story/the-common-current-bible.md`](story/the-common-current-bible.md),
reconciled in [`docs/story-bible.md`](story-bible.md); both merged in PR #735). It keeps the canon's premise,
regions, core cast, endings and reveal ladder, and adds the decisions below. `docs/story-bible.md` lists them in
short under its owner decisions; this page holds the full record.

## The story in one breath

A stone village's deliveries stop. The heroes bring its people home and then drive the village's delivery cart
after the machinery that trapped them: across a bay, into a bright, growing city, and up three valleys. Each
valley is better off for a public-works network, and each is quietly erased from its map. The engineer who built
that network is someone they fought beside before they knew her name. In the end they stop a storm inside the
machine without breaking it, and hand its controls to the people who live with it.

**Central question (canon):** can people share power without giving one person the right to choose everyone
else's life?
**Emotional destination (canon):** the heroes become people who make room for others to act.

## The writing standard (owner, 2026-11-14)

**A family game is not a lesser game.** Write to the standard of the best all-ages stories, the kind adults
rewatch for themselves. Do not soften writing to appease younger players:

- **Full emotional range.** Grief, guilt, anger, betrayal and moral compromise get room to land. The canon's tone
  limits (no gore, no torture, no child-death stakes) are content limits, not limits on depth.
- **Antagonists with a real case.** Veyra must be right about enough that a thoughtful adult half agrees with
  her. Rusk is frightening because he is calm and reasonable-sounding, not because he is loud.
- **Unresolved is allowed.** Some choices have no clean answer, some apologies are not accepted, and some
  relationships end the game still mending.
- **Subtext over explanation.** Kids read subtext better than writers assume. Never add a line that explains what
  a scene already showed.
- **Vocabulary and ideas at full strength.** Precise words, real institutions, real arguments. A child who misses
  a layer the first time finds it on the replay; that is a feature.
- **Kid-friendliness lives in the interface, not the story.** Big text, clear turns, forgiving difficulty and
  tap targets (CLAUDE.md) are how the game welcomes kids. The writing does not pay that cost.

## How the themes stay unspoken

Never stated (canon rule: no morals, no "what we learned"). Each theme has a recurring **image** that carries it:

| Theme          | Carried by                                                                    | Recurring image                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Responsibility | Returning after a rescue; signing your name on a decision; repair that costs  | The cart: mended, loaded, driven back               |
| Equality       | Doors, steps, keys and schedules that leave someone out, never a speech       | The assembly platform with only stairs, then a ramp |
| Fairness       | Who does the work, who gets paid, who gets asked, who gets sent               | The two bowls: one to eat, one to carry to someone  |
| Balance        | Movement that is cared for, not frozen; power shared, not seized or abandoned | Water that says things twice until it is let go     |

## Decisions (owner, 2026-11-14)

### Shape

1. **Title: _Four Nations Tactics: Echoes of Stillwater_.** Replaces the working title "The Common Current" (which
   stays the network's in-world name). Nothing with "Tide" in it: the owner has another game called Tidewrack.
2. **Depth: sharpen the spine.** Keep the canon; add throughlines, spectacle and earlier antagonist presence.
3. **Flash-forward prologue.** About ninety seconds inside the Stillwater Works in the storm: floodwater, a
   screaming turbine, a woman's hand on a valve, water rippling twice. No names, no faces. Cut to a quiet morning
   in Ba Dan with bowls set out. The saga plays as "how did we get here".
4. **Civic beats become set-pieces.** Every hearing, archive or assembly beat is staged as physical play: a chase,
   a heist, a crowd rescue, a race against a seizure squad. This is dramatic craft, not simplification. The
   argument itself stays whole: when the assembly debates, the other side's best case is heard in full.
5. **A fixed low point.** After the archive heist (MQ-09), Rusk posts the party as saboteurs, impounds the cart and
   holds Parcel as "evidence". Act IV opens on foot, sheltered by people the party helped earlier. Winning the
   cart back is part of the assembly. Who hides you is the theme.
6. **Three distinct endings** (canon E-01 to E-03), chosen by a final table vote, each with its own closing
   sequence.
7. **Final battle: the Engine in revolt.** A multi-phase fight against the Stillwater Engine as a living
   battlefield. You win by opening the route and handing over the controls, not by destroying anything.

### The cart

8. **Bo-shan's cart is the party's spine,** pulled by **Parcel the ostrich horse** (canon).
9. **Bo-shan drives it.** He gets the bigger life he wanted (canon), and it costs Pella, who stays home angry and
   proud. He is never a fighter; he is at every camp. Their MQ-11 reunion lands on a choice he made, not a rescue.
10. **On the Driller-loss branch, Dorin drives** (owner had no preference; Claude's call). He leaves his post with
    a travel permit he wrote himself. Canon's arc for him (learning discretion, reporting his own errors) gets road
    time. When Bo-shan is freed in MQ-11, Dorin hands back the reins. On this branch Gao patches the cart until
    Grumbler surrenders in MQ-11.
11. **Grumbler maintains the cart under supervision.** From Act II he works restitution in Ba Dan's workshop and
    makes one upgrade per act, complaining about every bolt. He is not forgiven. Pella keeps a ledger of what he
    owes the workers, and he pays it line by line across the saga.
12. **Upgrades are story and camp only.** A canopy, a ramp for a passenger who uses a wheelchair, a water barrel
    for the shelters, a lamp. No stats, no shop. The cart gets better at carrying other people.
13. **Latch rides the tailgate after MQ-07.** The tailgate is a threshold. Latch "collects" what is left on it,
    learning the difference between lost, given and borrowed across Acts III–V. Latch is never a pet: it can
    leave, and once it does.

### People

14. **Meet Veyra before you know who she is.** At Saltstep (MQ-02) the party works beside a capable, funny engineer
    named Veyra who mends her own teapot and hauls a pump with them. She never lies; nobody asks her job.
15. **Veyra fights beside the party once.** One guest fight at the Saltstep docks (MQ-02), AI-controlled, a
    non-bender with engineering tools (a pressure lance, a signal flare; canon §9 limits apply). If she gives up
    command in the finale, she rejoins with the same kit. **The guest fight is in the trial region** (Saltstep and
    Emberwatch, MQ-02; owner, 2026-11-14). The guest-ally system is new engine work that needs its own ADR, and it
    doubles as the pipeline proof's test that a region can add a mechanic cleanly
    ([pipeline-proof-plan.md](pipeline-proof-plan.md#6-proof-trial)).
16. **The reveal ladder holds.** At Emberwatch (MQ-03) the supply-control order is signed **"V. Sen, Director."**
    The festival (MQ-04) puts her face to the name. Nothing about her role is known before MQ-03.
17. **Veyra's ending target: accountable, still useful.** In every ending she has given up command and testified.
    In the epilogue she repairs a public pump under someone else's authority, perhaps the worker whose warning she
    brushed off at the festival. Not forgiveness, not ruin.
18. **Rusk was the flood official.** Seven years ago, in the Blackreed Flood, Rusk was the council officer who held
    rescue equipment behind paperwork, the exact failure Veyra founded Commonworks to end. She later hired him
    because he "gets things done now". In Act IV she realizes she rebuilt the thing she hated. (Compatible with
    canon's flood history; it adds who held the equipment.)
19. **Jin is the recurring rival.** Polite, exact, always with terms. Once per act (table below). Never redeemed for
    free.
20. **Mira's flaw lands through the messengers.** In MQ-11 it surfaces that all four messengers Mira sent into
    danger were hired workers and newcomers; no old-family child went. She did not decide it consciously, and
    that is the point. She admits it at her own table. Afterwards the notice board is written in more than one
    hand. The messengers keep the names and jobs approved earlier that day, each with an outsider's or hired
    background: **Leto**, a cart driver hired from the coast; **Amri**, the letter walker who argues with Mira,
    who came from Saltstep about five years ago; **Hesra**, a hill-farm girl apprenticed into Ba Dan as a mason;
    **Senn**, a quarry-company tallyman who stayed after the company left. Act I plants this quietly (a stray
    detail each, never remarked on); MQ-11 lands it. The bible's messenger entry
    ([story-bible.md](story-bible.md#gap-fills-approved-2026-11-14), gap-fill 17) records the backgrounds.
21. **Ruon: repair without rank.** His MQ-11 testimony names the harmed workers before his reasons. In Act V he
    leads a brace crew on one front under the authority of a worker he once guarded. The epilogue shows him fitting
    boots for that crew. He never gets his captaincy back, and Mira never embraces him.
22. **Each hero brings their own reason**, found in Act I and paid off by their canon personal quest (table
    below). A party-neutral reason always exists, as canon requires for one to six heroes.

### The spirit thread

23. **Seeds in Act I.** In the quarry culvert, water ripples twice for one dropped stone. Later a worker's lost boot
    turns up on a Ba Dan doorstep with a river shell inside it. Nobody explains either.
24. **The Still Keeper is heard only as echoes before Act III.** At each region's water, something someone just said
    repeats back faintly: a child's laugh, Pella's question, a single word. Unexplained, slightly eerie, never
    threatening. In MQ-07 the party learns it has been listening the whole time, trying to keep those moments
    from changing.

## Major story beats

Each act ends on a quiet meal and a view (canon tone rule: follow intensity with food, a view or a safe talk).

### Prologue

0. **The storm inside the machine.** Floodwater, a turbine, a hand on a valve, water rippling twice. Cut to black,
   then to morning in Ba Dan.

### Act I: Stone (the shipped demo, MQ-01)

1. **The empty road.** Ba Dan without cart wheels; bowls set out for workers who may still come home. Pella wants
   her brother named.
2. **Pressed into violence.** The forest road attackers are quarry workers with stone dust on their cuffs.
3. **The gate.** Knock, or talk through by element, or (new, canon) through pay records or a worker's
   introduction, so every party has a real route.
4. **What do you owe someone who surrenders?** Ruon: escort for trial, or trade to Jin.
5. **The Cutting.** Kuan's tea stop. _Seed:_ water ripples twice in the culvert.
6. **The Driller.** Grumbler's imported machine, a Republic City maker's plate. Victory frees the workers; a loss
   keeps the quarry occupied and the saga continues.
7. **Evidence.** Off-ledger cargo marks; each present hero finds their reason.
8. **Welcome home.** Bo-shan and Pella. _Seed:_ a lost boot on a doorstep, with a shell inside. The cart is
   repaired; Bo-shan takes the reins and Pella will not come out to wave. Across the bay, Emberwatch's dead lamps
   come on.

### Act II: Sparks (MQ-02 to MQ-05)

9. **Paper and Stone (MQ-02).** The cart's first run, to Saltstep's floating market. Chase a customs stall that
   drifts off mid-argument. The party meets Veyra on the docks and fights beside her once. A wage envelope in a
   crate marked "safety materials". _Echo:_ the harbour water repeats Bo-shan's laugh. The table votes: accuse
   publicly or investigate quietly.
10. **Lamps Across the Bay (MQ-03).** Emberwatch: beans drying on the parade ground, a real clinic, mechanics.
    _Set-piece:_ a night infiltration through the rain cistern, or a bluffed safety inspection. In the dispatch
    room, the order is signed "V. Sen, Director". **Twist one.** Jin offers to sell the manifest.
11. **The Day the Lights Stayed On (MQ-04).** Causeway's festival in full joy: baths, music, a lift a child can work.
    A demonstration gate jams and Veyra wades in to save a worker. Then she brushes off another worker's warning.
    She knows the party saw both. She offers them an inspection commission.
12. **Three Routes, One Promise (MQ-05).** _Set-piece:_ a race through the tram yards and dispatch office before
    records are moved. Three erased places appear: a train stop, a marsh route, a bell. The five teachers are
    offered. Over a shared meal, the table picks the order of the spokes. Grumbler's first upgrade: a canopy.

### Act III: Currents (MQ-06 to MQ-09; spokes in any order)

13. **The Train That Forgot a Town (MQ-06).** Copper Orchard. A runaway maintenance wagon among fruit crates. A
    grandmother's settlement becomes an "unscheduled stop". The old guild excluded people too; neither side is
    clean. (Bo's and Riko's personal quests.)
14. **A River with No Reflection (MQ-07).** Reedglass. The water flows upstream in a loop and the school bell rings
    twice. **The seeds pay off:** Latch returns the other boot, then climbs onto the tailgate. Auntie Silt, then an
    audience with the Still Keeper, who was promised "no more sudden changes" and has been keeping every echo.
    (Sura, Lin Mei, Nilak.)
15. **The Mountain That Wouldn't Listen (MQ-08).** Bellweather. A stalled cable tram, a goose honking over the
    warning, a bell code the lower camps never understood. A line of flags answers across the valley. (Nima,
    Jinu.)
16. **The Map Under the Map (MQ-09).** _Set-piece heist_ in the maintenance archive, with Rusk's seizure squad
    sweeping the floors and Jin copying the same files. The overlay shows the erased people and the blocked spirit
    route are the same omission. Veyra refused a shutdown after she knew. **Twist two.** The table votes: publish
    now, or protect workers first.
17. **The fall.** Either way, Rusk moves first. The party is posted as saboteurs; the cart is impounded and Parcel
    held as "evidence". Latch leaves. The act ends on foot, in the rain, at a door the party once helped.

### Act IV: Weight (MQ-10 to MQ-12)

18. **Sheltered.** Whoever the party helped hides them: Tavi's ferry, the Six-Ladle Kitchen, a rail crew. With
    little optional help, a stranger does it anyway. Veyra learns who held the flood equipment seven years ago:
    the man running her security.
19. **Everyone in the Room (MQ-10).** The assembly, whose speaking platform has only stairs. A pump floods the lower
    landing; Rusk uses it to seize the hall. _Set-piece:_ a crowd rescue while the party (or locals) builds a ramp
    mid-battle so the people below can be heard. Nobody comments on the ramp. The cart and Parcel are won back.
20. **Back to the Stone (MQ-11).** Ba Dan again, changed by Act I's choices. Pella and Bo-shan, at last, with her
    anger as well as her joy. Ruon's testimony names the workers first. Mira admits who she sent. The village
    decides for itself whether to send a crew. A chair at Mira's table is finally used. Latch is back on the
    tailgate, holding a shell it insists was given.
21. **The Night Before the Tide (MQ-12).** Camp conversations, one per selected hero, and a meal. Every remaining
    door is named before the final commitment. The first rain falls only after the table commits.

### Act V: Balance (MQ-13 to MQ-15)

22. **Three Ways to Hold a Night (MQ-13).** Three fronts; the party plays one in full, and allies from earlier acts
    carry the others. Ruon leads a brace crew under a worker he once guarded. **Rusk's fight** is at the spillway.
    He is an obstacle, not the last argument.
23. **The Engine in Revolt (MQ-14), the final battle.** The prologue, now with names and faces.
    - **Phase 1, the flood floor:** the Engine's gates and turbines shift the battlefield; Rusk's last loyalists
      hold the controls.
    - **Phase 2, the echo:** the Still Keeper's repeating current takes over the machine, and every action echoes
      on the next round, along with every echo the party has heard since Act I.
    - **Phase 3, letting go:** if earned, Veyra gives up command, rejoins as a guest, and works the valve from the
      prologue. The party protects the operators while it secures canon's four practical facts: a relief route
      open, operators able to stop the system, downstream warned, the spirit passage unsealed.
    - **The vote:** the table chooses E-01 The Shared Current, E-02 The Stewardship Winter, or E-03 Many Small
      Lights. Each has its own closing sequence.
24. **What Happens Tomorrow (MQ-15).** Individual outcomes: Veyra repairing a pump under someone else's authority,
    Ruon fitting boots, Grumbler's ledger paid, Jin's exit on the letter of her terms. One personal closing scene
    for each selected hero. **Final image:** Bo-shan stops the cart at a settlement that was erased from the map
    and asks if anyone has a letter, a repair, or room for one more. At Mira's table, someone moves a chair so
    someone else can sit. The water outside says nothing twice.

## Risks and trade-offs

- **Scope.** Fifteen missions, ten personal quests, thirty side stories and three endings make 30–40 hours of
  writing and art. The endings roughly triple Act V's closing work. Author them last, once the region pipeline's
  cost per region is measured.
- **The echo phase and guest units may need engine work.** "Every action echoes next round" is likely a new
  effect kind, and an AI-controlled guest may be new to the encounter data. Both touch `src/core/` and need an
  ADR. Prove the echo on a small Act III encounter (MQ-07) before the finale relies on it; the guest is proved
  at MQ-02, inside the trial region (decision 15).
- **The fall must not undo choices.** Impounding the cart is fixed; it must not erase side-story progress or punish
  the publish-or-protect vote (canon says both are justified).
- **Veyra in MQ-02 is presence, not a reveal.** Her name, not her role, is known early. No line may hint at her
  title before MQ-03.
- **The loss branch now carries more.** Dorin as driver, Gao's repairs and Grumbler's late surrender all need
  their own lines; the content tests that enumerate routes must cover them.
- **Dense scenes.** The cure for a slow scene is better writing (sharper conflict, a cut line, a scene that is
  staged instead of explained), never a simpler idea or a reading-time cap.
