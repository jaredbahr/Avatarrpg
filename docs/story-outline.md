# Saga outline: the spine of the story

**Status: proposed (2026-11-14), waiting for the owner's yes.** This page sharpens the approved canon
([`docs/story/the-common-current-bible.md`](story/the-common-current-bible.md), reconciled in
[`docs/story-bible.md`](story-bible.md), both from PR #735). It changes no canon premise, region, cast member or
reveal-ladder step. It adds seven owner-chosen upgrades (below) and lays the major beats out in one place. Once
approved, its decisions move into `docs/story-bible.md` under a dated "Owner decisions" list.

## The story in one breath

A stone village's deliveries stop. The heroes bring its people home and then follow the machinery that trapped
them across a bay, into a bright, growing city, and up three valleys. Each valley is better off for a public-works
network, and each is quietly erased from its map. The engineer who built that network is someone they liked
before they knew her name. In the end they stop a storm inside the machine without breaking it. Then they hand its
controls to the people who live with it.

**Central question (canon):** can people share power without giving one person the right to choose everyone
else's life?
**Emotional destination (canon):** the heroes become people who make room for others to act.

## How the themes stay unspoken

Never stated (canon rule: no morals, no "what we learned"). Each theme has a recurring **image** that carries it:

| Theme          | Carried by                                                                    | Recurring image                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Responsibility | Returning after a rescue; signing your name on a decision; repair that costs  | Bo-shan's cart: mended, loaded, driven back         |
| Equality       | Doors, steps, keys and schedules that leave someone out, never a speech       | The assembly platform with only stairs, then a ramp |
| Fairness       | Who does the work, who gets paid, who gets asked                              | The two bowls: one to eat, one to carry to someone  |
| Balance        | Movement that is cared for, not frozen; power shared, not seized or abandoned | Water that "says things twice" until it is let go   |

## The writing standard (owner, 2026-11-14)

**A family game is not a lesser game.** Write to the standard of the best all-ages stories, the kind adults
rewatch for themselves. Do not soften writing to appease younger players:

- **Full emotional range.** Grief, guilt, anger, betrayal and moral compromise get room to land. The canon's tone
  limits (no gore, no torture, no child-death stakes) are content limits, not limits on depth.
- **Antagonists with a real case.** Veyra must be right about enough that a thoughtful adult half agrees with
  her. Rusk is frightening because he is calm and reasonable-sounding, not because he is loud.
- **Unresolved is allowed.** Some choices have no clean answer, some apologies are not accepted, and some
  relationships end the game still mending.
- **Subtext over explanation.** Kids read subtext better than writers assume. Never add a line that explains what
  a scene already showed.
- **Vocabulary and ideas at full strength.** Precise words, real institutions, real arguments. A child who misses
  a layer the first time finds it on the replay; that is a feature.
- **Kid-friendliness lives in the interface, not the story.** Big text, clear turns, forgiving difficulty and
  tap targets (CLAUDE.md) are how the game welcomes kids. The writing does not pay that cost.

## Owner-chosen upgrades (2026-11-14)

1. **Depth: sharpen the spine.** Keep the canon's premise, regions, cast and endings. Add throughlines,
   spectacle and earlier antagonist presence.
2. **Meet Veyra before you know who she is.** In Saltstep (MQ-02) the party works beside a capable, funny
   engineer named Veyra who mends her own teapot and helps them haul a pump. She never lies; nobody asks her job.
   At Emberwatch (MQ-03) the supply-control order is signed **"V. Sen, Director."** The festival (MQ-04) puts her
   face to the name. The reveal ladder is unchanged: nothing about her role is known before MQ-03.
3. **Jin as the recurring rival.** Polite, exact, always with terms. She appears once per act. She is sometimes
   an obstacle and sometimes a paid ally, and she is never redeemed for free (beats below).
4. **Seed the spirit mystery in Act I.** In the quarry culvert, water ripples twice for one dropped stone.
   Later a worker's lost boot turns up on a Ba Dan doorstep with a river shell inside it. Nobody explains either.
   Act III does: the echo is the Still Keeper's repeated current, and the boot is Latch, who mistakes "lost" for
   "unclaimed".
5. **Civic beats become set-pieces.** Every hearing, archive or assembly beat is staged as physical play: a chase,
   a heist, a crowd rescue, a race against a seizure squad. This is dramatic craft, not simplification: the theme
   lives in what people do. The argument itself stays whole. When the assembly debates, the debate is real, the
   other side's best case is heard in full, and it takes as long as it needs.
6. **The cart is the party's spine.** Bo-shan's produce cart, pulled by **Parcel the ostrich horse** (canon),
   is repaired after Act I. It becomes the party's caravan, and it gains one practical upgrade per act. The
   canon's final image, a public cart stopping at a town erased from the map, is that cart.
7. **Each hero brings their own reason**, found in Act I's evidence and paid off by their canon personal quest.
   A party-neutral reason always exists (Pella and the cart), as canon requires for one-to-six heroes.
8. **Three distinct endings** (canon E-01 to E-03), chosen by a final table vote with distinct closing sequences.
9. **Final battle: the Engine in revolt.** A multi-phase fight against the Stillwater Engine as a living
   battlefield. You win by opening the route and handing over the controls, not by destroying anything.

## Major story beats

Each act ends on a quiet meal and a view (canon tone rule: follow intensity with food, a view or a safe talk).

### Act I: Stone (the shipped demo, MQ-01)

1. **The empty road.** Ba Dan without cart wheels; bowls set out for workers who may still come home. Pella wants
   her brother named.
2. **Pressed into violence.** The forest road attackers are quarry workers with stone dust on their cuffs.
3. **The gate.** Knock, or talk through by element, or (new, canon) through pay records or a worker's
   introduction, so every party has a real route.
4. **What do you owe someone who surrenders?** Ruon: escort for trial, or trade to Jin.
5. **The Cutting.** Sen's tea stop (renamed Kuan in canon). _Seed:_ water ripples twice in the culvert.
6. **The Driller.** Grumbler's imported machine, a Republic City maker's plate. Victory frees the workers; a
   loss keeps the quarry occupied and the saga continues.
7. **Evidence.** Off-ledger cargo marks; each present hero finds their reason (table above).
8. **Welcome home.** Bo-shan and Pella. _Seed:_ a lost boot on a doorstep, with a shell inside. The party
   repairs the cart. Across the bay, Emberwatch's dead lamps come on.

### Act II: Sparks (MQ-02 to MQ-05)

9. **Paper and Stone (MQ-02).** The cart's first run to Saltstep's floating market. Chase a customs stall that
   drifts off mid-argument. A wage envelope in a crate marked "safety materials". _New:_ the party meets Veyra
   at the docks, hauling a pump with them. The table votes: accuse publicly or investigate quietly.
10. **Lamps Across the Bay (MQ-03).** Emberwatch: beans drying on the parade ground, a real clinic, mechanics.
    _Set-piece:_ a night infiltration through the rain cistern, or a bluffed safety inspection. In the dispatch
    room, the order is signed "V. Sen, Director". **Twist one.** Jin offers to sell the manifest.
11. **The Day the Lights Stayed On (MQ-04).** Causeway's festival in full joy: baths, music, a lift a child can
    work. A demonstration gate jams and Veyra wades in beside the party to save a worker. Then she brushes off a
    frightened worker's warning. She offers the party an inspection commission.
12. **Three Routes, One Promise (MQ-05).** _Set-piece:_ a race through the tram yards and dispatch office
    before records are moved. Three erased places appear: a train stop, a marsh route, a bell. The five teachers
    are offered. Over a shared meal, the table picks the order of the spokes. The cart gains its first upgrade.

### Act III: Currents (MQ-06 to MQ-09; spokes in any order)

13. **The Train That Forgot a Town (MQ-06).** Copper Orchard. A runaway maintenance wagon among fruit crates. A
    grandmother's settlement becomes an "unscheduled stop". The old guild excluded people too; neither side is
    clean. (Bo's and Riko's personal quests.)
14. **A River with No Reflection (MQ-07).** Reedglass. The water flows upstream in a loop and the school bell
    rings twice. **Payoff of the Act I seed:** Latch returns the boot's owner's other shoe. Auntie Silt, then
    an audience with the Still Keeper, who was promised "no more sudden changes". (Sura, Lin Mei, Nilak.)
15. **The Mountain That Wouldn't Listen (MQ-08).** Bellweather. A stalled cable tram, a goose honking over the
    warning, a bell code the lower camps never understood. A line of flags answers across the valley. (Nima,
    Jinu.)
16. **The Map Under the Map (MQ-09).** _Set-piece heist_ in the maintenance archive, with Rusk's seizure squad
    sweeping the floors and Jin copying the same files. The overlay shows the erased people and the blocked
    spirit route are the same omission. Veyra refused a shutdown after she knew. **Twist two.** The table votes:
    publish now, or protect workers first.

### Act IV: Weight (MQ-10 to MQ-12)

17. **Everyone in the Room (MQ-10).** The assembly, whose speaking platform has only stairs. A pump floods the
    lower landing; Rusk uses it to seize the hall. _Set-piece:_ a crowd rescue while the party (or locals)
    builds a ramp mid-battle so the people below can be heard. Nobody comments on the ramp.
18. **Back to the Stone (MQ-11).** Ba Dan again, changed by Act I's choices. Ruon's account is heard. Bo-shan's
    reunion with Pella holds her anger as well as her joy. The village decides for itself whether to send a
    crew. A chair at Mira's table is finally used.
19. **The Night Before the Tide (MQ-12).** Camp conversations, one per selected hero, and a meal. Every
    remaining door is named before the final commitment. The first rain falls only after the table commits.

### Act V: Balance (MQ-13 to MQ-15)

20. **Three Ways to Hold a Night (MQ-13).** Three fronts; the party plays one in full, and allies named in earlier
    acts carry the others. **Rusk's fight** is at the spillway. He is an obstacle, not the last argument.
21. **The Engine in Revolt (MQ-14), the final battle.**
    - **Phase 1, the flood floor:** the Engine's gates and turbines shift the battlefield; Rusk's last loyalists
      hold the controls.
    - **Phase 2, the echo:** the Still Keeper's repeating current takes over the machine, and every action
      echoes on the next round. The Act I seed was always this.
    - **Phase 3, letting go:** if earned, Veyra gives up command mid-fight and works a valve. The party protects
      the operators while it secures canon's four practical facts: a relief route open, operators able to stop
      the system, downstream warned, and the spirit passage unsealed. You win by opening the route, not by
      destroying anything.
    - **The vote:** the table chooses E-01 The Shared Current, E-02 The Stewardship Winter, or E-03 Many Small
      Lights. Each has its own closing sequence.
22. **What Happens Tomorrow (MQ-15).** Individual outcomes for Veyra, Rusk, Jin, Ruon and Grumbler; one
    personal closing scene for each selected hero. **Final image:** the party's own cart stops at a settlement
    that was erased from the map. The driver asks if anyone has a letter, a repair, or room for one more. At
    Mira's table, someone moves a chair so someone else can sit.

## Risks and trade-offs (flagged for the owner)

- **Scope.** Fifteen missions, ten personal quests, thirty side stories and three endings make 30–40 hours of
  writing and art. The three endings roughly triple Act V's closing work. Author the endings last, once the
  region pipeline's cost per region is measured.
- **The echo phase may need engine work.** "Every action echoes next round" is likely a new effect kind, so it
  touches `src/core/` and needs an ADR. Prove it on a small Act III encounter (MQ-07) before the finale relies on it.
- **Dense scenes.** The cure for a slow scene is better writing (sharper conflict, a cut line, a scene that is
  staged instead of explained), never a simpler idea or a reading-time cap.
- **Veyra in MQ-02 is new presence, not a new reveal.** Her name, not her role, is known early. Writers must not
  give her lines that hint at her title before MQ-03.
- **Hooks for a party of one.** Each hero's reason must work alone. The neutral cart thread must carry the story
  for any party, and it must never feel like a consolation prize.
