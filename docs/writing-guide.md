# Dialogue and humor

Four Nations Tactics should be enjoyable company. Its people have work to do,
things they want, relationships, and reasons to be funny. Their voices should
remain distinct when the nameplates are hidden.

This guide applies to runtime dialogue, narration, biographies, incidental
discoveries, and future story drafts. It accompanies the Common Current story
bible and its living-world expansion. The owner's direction takes priority.

## Build the situation first

Give someone an immediate want: serve dinner, finish a model, win a harmless
competition, impress a visitor, or conceal a small embarrassment. Let their
specific approach create the comic turn. They need not know they are being funny.

Allow broad comedy, expressive reactions, stubborn pride, physical mishaps, and
escalation when the scene earns them. Warmth does not require everyone to be
quiet or sensible. Keep the consequences appropriate to the moment.

A concrete example in the current opening: Dorin tells Gao a water skin leaks.
Gao fills it and waits with him to see the leak. Dorin eventually asks for more
water. The exchange depends on these neighbors and a small embarrassment; it
does not need the narrator to explain the joke.

For future staged scenes, Wen can demonstrate an improved stove handle that
works perfectly until Tenzo puts his cooking pot over it. The pot exposes the
omission. She fixes the handle while the stove is cold. Her expertise survives
the joke, and Tenzo does not need a string of sarcastic replies.

Make bending part of ordinary work: cooking, surveying, fishing, hauling stone.
Give non-benders useful skills and commitments of their own. Industrial machinery
should affect the people who work around it and the water they drink. Let kindness
cost a little effort: washing a cup, waiting for a straggler, feeding another person.
The action can carry the meaning.

## Write people responding to each other

- Most turns should fit comfortably within 35 words. Split long explanations
  at meaningful beats rather than padding them with jokes.
- Vary the rhythm. A reply may be an action, useful information, an unfinished
  thought, or silence. Do not make every exchange four alternating punchlines.
- Read it aloud. Would this person actually say it to this listener now?
- Stop at the strongest beat. Remove the extra comeback and the narrator's
  explanation of why the situation is funny.
- Let callbacks develop a shared experience. Repeating a template with a new
  noun is not development.
- Let sincere lines remain sincere. A reunion, surrender, defeat, or confession
  does not need a punchline to keep the audience interested.

Watch for interchangeable filler: “Well, that happened,” “That's reassuring,”
“Technically…,” “I meant to do that,” and “which is somehow worse.” Also watch
for sarcastic objects, elaborate bureaucracy for every animal, mock philosophy
about chores, and a narrator who keeps grading the player's misfortune. These
patterns call for editorial judgment, not a banned-word checker.

## Keep the fiction honest

The setting is after Korra and before Seven Havens; the exact year remains
reserved. Preserve established Avatar lore and the original cast. Humor does
not justify inventing a species, power, national stereotype, or familiar
franchise scene. New canon claims need their own reference review.

Use only present speakers and facts they can know. A party can contain one to
six heroes. Pella stays in Ba Dan during the quarry mission. Bo and Bo-shan are
different people; Bo-shan is missing in addition to Mira's four messengers.

Defeat text must work regardless of which attacks occurred. Never assume an oil
explosion, lost eyebrows, or a particular party composition. Respect the current
custody and rescue outcome before selecting ordinary social dialogue.

Narration describes observable details. A cup, nest, or repaired chair does not
need an accompanying statement of the campaign's theme. An optional discovery
cannot assume that the player already heard another optional conversation.

Keep journal entries within what the player has already learned. A first-visit
flag cannot establish something that happens only on a later visit. Check tactical
claims against the actual rules; humour must not promise an unimplemented reward,
status effect or shop mechanic. Choice descriptions state consequences clearly;
NPCs may judge a decision without the UI declaring the correct moral answer.

Stage directions in the bible are production requests, not implemented animation.
When a comic action is essential, either stage it or describe it clearly through
an appropriate narrator. Do not put unmarked actions into a character's speech.
Keep node IDs, destinations, conditions, rewards, and interlude shot counts intact
unless the task explicitly requires a behavior change.

## Review before integration

Identify the immediate want and the cause of the comic turn. Hide the names and
check the voices. Cut a redundant final tag. Read the exchange aloud and check
its branch facts. If removing the joke improves the scene, remove it.

Use the project's normal validation and review gates. Tests protect story links,
choices, save compatibility, and presentation contracts; they cannot certify
that a joke works. Do not add exact-text tests merely to freeze an editorial
preference.
